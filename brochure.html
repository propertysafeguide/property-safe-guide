The Red Flag Field Manual

Four examples a rural deal quietly turns into a liability.

These aren't hypotheticals — they're the anomalies that surface on real Schedule Bs, county aerials, and survey plats across Kaufman, Parker, and outer Collin County.

01
Unrecorded

The family cemetery trap

Descendants keep a permanent legal right to visit a family burial plot — undisclosed, unmapped, and a liability for the agent, the title company, and the surveyor who each signed off on the file.

02
No RMA on file

The private road quagmire

A shared gravel road with no Road Maintenance Agreement is an access risk for lenders, an underwriting flag for insurers, and a future dispute for the attorney who didn't catch it.

03
Schedule B, vague

The blanket easement

A decades-old utility easement with no mapped coordinates can let a co-op bulldoze straight through a new structure — a mapping problem for the engineer and a claims problem for the insurer.

04
$15k–$35k risk

Septic & well failure

Standard inspections gloss over OSSF life-cycle and well yield — the failure surfaces right after closing, as an appraisal dispute or a warranty claim, not before.
